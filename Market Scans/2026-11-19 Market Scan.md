# Market Scan: Thu, Nov 19, 2026

> [!summary] The day in one line
> An early rally on Anthropic's Nasdaq debut flipped into a broad selloff: the S&P 500 fell 2.0% for a sixth straight loss, the Dow dropped about 1,100 points (2.2%) to a six-month low and the Nasdaq lost 2.3%, with [[AMD]] sliding about 8% on a short-seller report, [[NVDA]], [[AMZN]] and [[GOOGL]] down 3–4% and [[UNH]] off another 5.6%, while [[PANW]] led the S&P 500 higher after its beat-and-raise.

## What moved the market

- **Anthropic debut, then a reversal.** Anthropic (ANT) first traded around midday at $111.50, about 34% above its $83 IPO price, and closed at $97.17 (+17%). The S&P 500 had been up about 1.3% early but turned sharply lower, which Kiplinger called its biggest intraday reversal in a year; the debut did not lift the large Anthropic shareholders, with [[AMZN]] down about 3.9% and [[GOOGL]] about 3.5%.
- **Chips sold off.** The Philadelphia Semiconductor index fell about 2.4%, with 27 of its 30 members lower. [[AMD]] fell about 7.7% after Hunterbrook, which disclosed a short position, alleged blacklisted Chinese server maker IEIT kept getting AMD firmware; AMD said the report misrepresents its business. [[NVDA]] faded an early gain to close down 3.3% at $145.96, its sixth straight drop, and [[MRVL]] fell about 4%. [[INTC]] (+7.4%, after saying it signed a first outside 18A customer) and [[QCOM]] bucked the trend.
- **Insurers and health care.** [[UNH]] fell another 5.6% to $330.48, the Dow's biggest drag and about 20% lower in three sessions, as Sen. Sanders' Medicare for All push added to worries about the proposed Medicare Advantage audit rule. [[MRK]] and [[LLY]] also fell.
- **Fed and data.** The October Fed minutes showed nearly all officials expecting a December quarter-point hike, with two raising a half-point move, per Rock & Turner. The Philadelphia Fed's factory index dropped to 6.3 from 20.9, and weekly jobless claims were about 203,000 (StockTitan).
- **Consumer and speculative names** such as cruise lines, airlines and apparel retailers also fell; Williams-Sonoma (not in the vault) dropped about 15% after it did not raise its outlook.

## Notable company news

- **Earnings:** [[WMT]] beat on revenue ($188.8B, +5.2%) and adjusted EPS ($0.65) and raised full-year guidance a third time, but slower U.S. comparable sales and a rich valuation left shares about 1% lower. [[PANW]] rose about 6.4%, the S&P 500's top gainer, as most brokers raised targets after its beat-and-raise and Applied Compute deal. After the close: Intuit, Ross Stores, Gap, Alibaba and PDD (not in the vault).
- **Megacaps:** [[MSFT]] about -1.8% as Guggenheim reiterated Buy after Ignite; [[META]] -3.3%, a fourth straight loss; [[TSLA]] about -1.7% in the afternoon as public Cybercab rides started in Dallas; [[AAPL]] close not found.
- **Chips, more:** [[TSM]] fell 3.5% in Taipei as the Taiex slid 4.4%, with the FT reporting TSMC and Samsung paused some China shipments while seeking U.S. licenses; [[MU]] jumped about 6.6% at the open then turned lower; [[AMAT]] reversed an early gain after Bernstein named it its top chip-equipment pick; [[ASML]] rose about 2% in Amsterdam.
- **Staples:** [[PG]] laid out up to $10B of productivity savings through fiscal 2030 at its Investor Day; shares were about 1% lower during the event. [[KO]] fell about 3.1%, with Investing.com pointing to its highest valuation in about 25 years.
- **Other:** [[JPM]] said a cyberattack on third-party vendors may have exposed some customer names and emails. [[CSCO]] CEO Chuck Robbins sold about $27M of stock under a pre-set trading plan. [[CVX]] (+0.3%) was the only Dow gainer as oil edged up on Hormuz tensions; Erste upgraded [[XOM]] to Buy.
