# Market Scan: Fri, Nov 20, 2026

> [!summary] The day in one line
> The AI selloff turned into a rout: the Nasdaq sank 5.3% and wiped out its 2026 gain, the S&P 500 fell 3.7% for a seventh straight loss and joined the Dow (-1.9%) in a correction, as [[NVDA]] plunged about 14% on a reported SEC accounting review, [[AMD]], [[MU]] and [[AVGO]] fell 12–14% and Anthropic gave back 9%, while [[UNH]] and [[WMT]] led a defensive rotation; all three indexes had their worst week since April 2025.

## What moved the market

- **AI trade unwound.** Chips led the drop. The Philadelphia Semiconductor index lost about 10.7%, with every one of its 30 members lower. [[NVDA]] closed down about 14% at $125.53, its lowest close in about 18 months, after Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are looking at possible accounting issues (the SEC declined to comment, and a probe does not mean wrongdoing has been found). [[MU]] (-13.9%), [[AMD]] (-13.3%) and [[AVGO]] (-12.1%) also fell hard. Reports pointed to doubts that heavy AI and data-center spending will pay off, and AP also cited midterm results that favor slower data-center building.
- **Anthropic's second day.** Anthropic (ANT) fell about 9.1% to $88.30, per AP. That is still above its $83 IPO price but about 23% below Thursday's peak of $115.10, and TradingKey said it briefly traded near $83 during the day. Reuters (via MarketScreener) said the weak follow-through fed worries about the AI trade.
- **Global spillover.** Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each fell more than 5%, and Taiwan's Taiex dropped about 5.7%, on pace for its worst week since 1997 (StockTitan, citing CNBC). [[ASML]] fell about 6% in Europe. Bitcoin dropped below $60,000, hitting crypto miners and AI-infrastructure names.
- **Rotation to defense.** Consumer staples rose about 1% and health care and energy edged up. Advancers were heavily outnumbered, with about 2,345 NYSE stocks down against 518 up (Investing.com). Treasuries rallied, pulling the 10-year yield below 5%.
- **Fed and data.** Traders still price a near-certain quarter-point Fed hike in December (CME FedWatch, via Reuters). Fed Governor Lisa Cook said rates are too low to bring inflation under control and that waiting too long risks falling behind. The final University of Michigan sentiment reading rose to 51.5 from 48.0 in October. Its survey director said the people with the most stock reported weaker personal finances.

## Notable company news

- **Chips, more:** [[AMAT]] (about -10.8%), [[INTC]] (about -7.8%) and [[TXN]] (about -7.6%) fell with the group (TradingKey). [[TSM]] was down about 8.5% in the early afternoon, per StockTitan. Rep. John Moolenaar called for closer scrutiny of [[NVDA]] and [[AMD]] chip sales, per a CNBC headline. SanDisk, Seagate, CoreWeave, Nebius, Bloom Energy and Corning (none in the vault) also fell sharply.
- **Megacaps:** [[META]] about -5.9%, [[TSLA]] about -7.5%, [[AMZN]] about 4–5% lower (sources differ), [[MSFT]] about -2.7%, [[GOOGL]] about -2.6% and [[AAPL]] about -1.8%. Apart from Amazon, these figures come from a single closing-tape source (MarketAlleys). TradingKey said Google is reportedly looking to buy HBM memory for its TPUs directly, which Bernstein estimates could save about $50B.
- **Defensives:** [[UNH]] rose 2.8% to $339.85, the Dow's top gainer, after a TD Cowen upgrade (per MarketScreener; the details of the upgrade were not confirmed). [[WMT]] gained 1.5% to $127.95, and [[JNJ]] rose about 1%. General Mills, McCormick and Global Payments (+10%) were among the S&P 500's best performers (none in the vault).
- **Earnings reactions:** Intuit (not in the vault) fell about 11% even though results beat estimates, because its guidance came in below expectations (MarketScreener said it cut its revenue-growth forecast). Gap (not in the vault) rose about 12% by early afternoon after an earnings beat, even though comparable sales were flat and it lowered guidance. Its closing move was not confirmed. Ross Stores (not in the vault) was down about 5% early on soft guidance. PDD (not in the vault) slipped about 2% after a revenue miss.
- **Other:** [[CSCO]] fell nearly 5% (Breitbart) and [[JPM]] about 3% (single source). Goldman Sachs (-6.7%) and Caterpillar (-5%) were the Dow's biggest decliners in Investing.com's list. After the close, SanDisk, Synopsys and United Rentals (none in the vault) fell in extended trading.
