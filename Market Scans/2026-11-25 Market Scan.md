# Market Scan: Wed, Nov 25, 2026

> [!summary] The day in one line
> A morning rout that had the S&P 500 down about 3% and the Nasdaq more than 4% — fed by hotter-than-expected core inflation, new U.S. tariff proposals on 14 economies and another leg of the AI-chip selloff — mostly reversed by the close: the S&P 500 ended -0.25%, the Nasdaq -0.62%, and the Dow and small caps finished higher heading into Thanksgiving.

## What moved the market

- **Inflation ran hot.** October PCE prices rose 3.8% from a year earlier (in line), but core PCE was 3.3%, a tenth above forecasts. Futures now fully price a quarter-point Fed hike at the Dec 8–9 meeting, while odds of a third hike in 2027 fell to about one in three from roughly even on Tuesday (CME FedWatch via Vista Global).
- **Strong economy data.** Q3 GDP was revised up to a 4.3% annualized pace from 3.4%; personal income rose 0.7%; jobless claims fell to 189,000, below the ~200,000 expected; new home sales rose to a 701,000 pace.
- **Tariff proposals.** The U.S. Trade Representative proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies — up to 20% on China, with Taiwan, South Korea and Japan among the targets (sources differ on country-by-country rates; semiconductors, autos and metals reportedly excluded). These are proposals only: comments are due Jan 5 and hearings start Jan 25. Taiwan's Taiex fell 4.8%, giving back about half of Tuesday's record jump.
- **AI selloff, then recovery.** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell as much as 8% before closing down about 0.7%. [[NVDA]] closed -3.9% at $149.30 (down as much as ~9% intraday) as Treasury's deadline for its export-control answers passed without a public response; [[MU]] -4.5% at $601.25 after reports of weaker Chinese memory orders; [[AMD]] -1.8% and [[AVGO]] roughly flat after drops near 10% and 8% at the lows; [[MRVL]] -0.5%. Credit-default-swap spreads on Oracle, CoreWeave and Alphabet hit records, a sign of strain in AI financing.
- **Chip equipment hit by China worries.** [[AMAT]] fell 4.4% and Lam Research about 6% on concern the proposed MATCH Act could limit tool sales to China; [[ASML]] dropped 5.8% in Amsterdam; [[TSM]] ADRs -2.0%.
- **Defensives and value held up.** Health care and staples firmed again: [[ABT]] rose about 3.5%, [[UNH]] about 1.2%, and [[PG]] hit a 52-week high. Oil names gained with crude ([[CVX]] +1.1%).
- **Other movers (not in the vault):** HP +11.5% and Deere +4.4% after earnings; Dell +0.6% after a roughly 103% jump in quarterly revenue; Ambarella -19.8% on a weak outlook; Nutanix -15.2% on a CEO exit. Anthropic (ANT) closed at $82.79, just under its $83 IPO price.

## Notable company news

- **[[ADI]]:** Rebounded after Tuesday's 17% drop (its worst day since 1998, per 24/7 Wall St.) as analysts cut targets but mostly kept positive ratings — TD Cowen to $375, Wells Fargo to $420, KeyBanc to $450; Truist stayed at Hold.
- **[[MU]]:** The Motley Fool cited TrendForce reports that Chinese cloud firms trimmed 2027 memory orders; TradingKey said Goldman reportedly lowered long-range memory price forecasts.
- **[[AMAT]]:** UBS kept Buy but cut its target to $575 from $640.
- **[[TSLA]]:** Fell about 5% intraday after Reuters reported the EU vote on its driver-assistance software slipped to Dec 15 from Dec 1.
- **[[META]]:** Rose roughly 2.5–3.4% late in the day on reports of a $4.5B GPU-backed loan to CoreWeave for a Meta data center and talks with Amazon over its shopping agent.
- **[[AMZN]]:** Fell about 3–5% as Bernstein flagged softer cloud web-activity data; [[GOOGL]] closed -2.1% at $273.10; [[AAPL]] about flat.
- **[[PANW]]:** A former vice president filed a lawsuit alleging improperly booked sales (Bloomberg); the claims are unproven.
- **[[ACN]]:** Wedbush started coverage at Outperform with a $266 target.
- **[[WMT]]:** Flipkart CEO Kalyan Krishnamurthy is stepping down.
- **[[LLY]]:** Moody's upgraded Lilly's credit rating to Aa2.
- **[[AMGN]]:** Settled patent litigation with Bristol Myers Squibb and Ono over its Opdivo biosimilar, allowing a U.S. launch in October 2028 if approved.
- **[[PEP]]** and **[[PG]]:** BNP Paribas raised targets to $160 and $197.
