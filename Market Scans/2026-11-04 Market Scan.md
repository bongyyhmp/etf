# Market Scan: Wed, Nov 4, 2026

> [!summary] The day in one line
> Democrats won control of both the House and Senate, and stocks staged a relief rally — the Dow jumped about 3% and the S&P 500 had its best day in three months — led by megacap software and retail names, while oil sank below $95 and energy, defense and AI-hardware stocks fell.

## What moved the market

- **Midterm results.** Democrats are set to control both chambers of Congress, ending one-party control of Washington; analysts described a relief rally on expectations of gridlock, with President Trump saying he would veto Democratic bills. Some House races and a few Senate seats were still uncalled.
- **Megacap software and retail jumped.** [[MSFT]] rose about 7% to a 52-week high, [[AMZN]] about 7%, [[AAPL]] about 6% to a record intraday high, and [[GOOGL]] about 3.2%; some coverage tied the moves to bets that a Democratic Congress could slow AI data-center spending and help free cash flow.
- **AI hardware lagged.** Worries about possible new curbs on data-center construction hit chips: [[NVDA]] fell about 3.1% (the biggest drag on the S&P 500), [[MU]] about 6%, [[MRVL]] about 5%, and [[INTC]] and [[AMAT]] about 3–4%; CoreWeave dropped about 10%.
- **Oil tumbled.** Brent fell about 8.7% to $94.62, a two-month low, on the election result and signs of renewed US–Iran diplomacy; energy was the worst sector, with [[COP]] down about 5%, [[XOM]] down sharply and [[CVX]] about 4% lower.
- **Defense stocks slid.** Lockheed Martin fell about 7% and Northrop Grumman about 4.5% on doubts about defense budgets; [[PLTR]] also fell on the same worry.
- **Yields eased.** The 10-year Treasury yield slipped to about 4.88% from 4.95%.
- **Defensives and banks gained.** [[WMT]], [[PG]] and [[KO]] rose, with PG and KO at 52-week highs; [[JPM]] climbed about 4% as bank stocks rallied, and health insurers and hospitals (HCA +10%) jumped on policy relief.

## Notable company news

- **Qualcomm ([[QCOM]])** reported fiscal Q4 revenue of about $10.32B and adjusted EPS of $2.17 after the close, slightly above forecasts; auto revenue hit a record ~$1.99B while handset sales fell 26%. December-quarter revenue guidance ($10.4–11.2B) topped estimates but the EPS midpoint was a bit below; shares fell about 5% after hours after rising about 3.6% in the session.
- **AMD ([[AMD]])** gained about 3% after its strong report, well below early premarket gains, as CEO Lisa Su projected 2027 revenue growth above 80%; several firms raised targets.
- **Amgen ([[AMGN]])** reversed its after-hours dip and rose about 3.6% to a 52-week high, with Morgan Stanley and RBC raising targets.
- **AbbVie ([[ABBV]])** licensed an oral IBD drug candidate from Nxera for $100M upfront and up to $1.47B in total.
- **Meta ([[META]])** drew a reported record ~€53B of orders for its first euro bond sale.
- **Tesla ([[TSLA]])** fell about 3.3% on worries about scrutiny of Musk's companies; China-made Tesla sales dropped about 29% in October from September.
- **Lilly ([[LLY]])** rose about 2% after rival Novo raised its 2026 outlook again; **UnitedHealth ([[UNH]])** gained modestly as insurers rallied.
- **Berkshire ([[BRK-B]])** confirmed it will release Q3 results on Saturday, Nov 7.
- **Other movers (not in the vault):** Trump Media fell about 25%; Fidelity National Information Services dropped about 25% after its CFO left and a JPMorgan downgrade; Block and Stanley Black & Decker rose about 15%.
