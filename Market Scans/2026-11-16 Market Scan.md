# Market Scan: Mon, Nov 16, 2026

> [!summary] The day in one line
> A new Commerce Department rule tightening overseas access to advanced chips, on the eve of [[NVDA]]'s first report without Jensen Huang, sparked another chip rout ([[MU]] -7.6%, [[INTC]] -7.1%, [[MRVL]] -7.4%) that sank the Nasdaq 2.5% into correction territory and the S&P 500 1.9%; health insurers and staples like [[PG]] were the rare gainers.

## What moved the market

- **New chip export rule.** The Commerce Department issued an interim rule requiring licenses for advanced-chip shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil and limiting remote use of overseas data centers by companies from China and other restricted countries. The PHLX Semiconductor index slid more than 4%, and Taiwan's Taiex fell about 6%.
- **Nvidia jitters before earnings.** [[NVDA]] fell 4.1% to $156.14, about 35% below its October high, a day before its Q3 report. Treasury Secretary Bessent said over the weekend that the next CEO should come from outside the company. A petition and President Trump backed Huang's return, but Reuters reported the board is not considering it.
- **Broad selling, weak breadth.** Decliners outnumbered advancers about 3 to 1 on the NYSE and Nasdaq. Tech, oil & gas and industrials led the losses; Caterpillar fell about 5% and Boeing hit a 52-week low.
- **Macro.** The Empire State manufacturing index dropped to 7.8. FedWatch still priced roughly a 96% chance of a December rate hike.
- **Bank outages.** Online banking at Chase and several other big banks went down for part of the day, and big-bank shares fell about 2%.

## Notable company news

- **Chips:** [[MU]] -7.6% (a court let a DRAM price-fixing class action proceed), [[INTC]] -7.1% (a new securities class action), [[MRVL]] -7.4% even after Arete raised its target, [[AVGO]] -4.8% after Redburn cut it to Neutral, [[AMD]] -3.7% as it closed its World Labs deal. [[AMAT]] (about -4.9%), [[KLAC]] (-5.3%), [[ADI]] (-3.1%) and [[TXN]] (-3.2%) also fell. [[TSM]] dropped 5.7% in Taipei and [[ASML]] 2.4% in Amsterdam.
- **Megacaps:** [[GOOGL]] -3.5%, [[TSLA]] -3.4%, [[AMZN]] -3.2% (a failed ULA launch destroyed 40 Amazon Leo satellites), [[META]] about -2.4% (Italy fined it about €805M over privacy), [[PLTR]] -3.5%. [[AAPL]] and [[MSFT]] held up better, each under 1% lower at midday.
- **Security:** [[PANW]] fell about 3% two days before its report; TD Cowen kept its Buy rating.
- **Gainers:** [[PG]] rose 1.9% to $173.02, the Dow's best and a 52-week high, ahead of Thursday's Investor Day. [[UNH]] gained 1.1% to $412.02 as health insurers rallied after Medicaid's agency eased the timing of work-rule enforcement. [[KO]] topped $100 for the first time intraday.
- **Deals:** [[JNJ]] agreed to buy Ollin Biosciences for $2.2B upfront, mainly for a late-stage eye-disease antibody; [[LLY]] agreed to buy Calytrix Bio for up to $1.5B; the FT reported [[CVX]] is near a roughly $5B deal for a majority stake in Beacon Offshore Energy. [[XOM]] started production at Uaru in Guyana. Barclays raised its [[ABBV]] target to $335.
- **Berkshire 13F:** [[BRK-B]]'s third-quarter filing showed a new stake in Bending Spoons, a near-doubling of Lennar, and further trimming of Bank of America; its Coca-Cola and Chevron stakes were unchanged.
- **Energy:** [[XOM]], [[CVX]] and [[COP]] fell with crude.
- **Not in the vault:** Molina Healthcare jumped 11.3% and Centene 4.9%; Tyson Foods rose 8.7%; Teradyne fell 7.1%.
