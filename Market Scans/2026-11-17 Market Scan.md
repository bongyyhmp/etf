## What moved the market

- **Waiting on Nvidia.** The S&P 500 swung between roughly flat and a 1.7% loss before settling down about 1%, as investors braced for [[NVDA]]'s first report since Jensen Huang's exit. Nvidia trimmed an intraday drop of more than 3% to close about 0.4% lower near $155.46.
- **Nvidia beats after the bell.** Q3 revenue came in near $115.8B against about $108.8B expected, adjusted EPS $2.68 vs. $2.47, and data-center revenue about $107.6B. It guided Q4 revenue to about $130B (±3%), above a ~$124.9B consensus; shares rose about 1.6% after hours.
- **Software and insurers sold off.** Salesforce (-9.5%), Intuit (-7.2%) and ServiceNow (-6.5%) led a software slide, and health insurers fell after CMS proposed an anti-fraud rule expanding Medicare Advantage audits. Financials, utilities and health care led sector losses.
- **Macro.** An underlying measure of October retail sales came in stronger than expected, lifting yields early before a move into safe assets reversed it. Oil was mixed as tanker attacks near Hormuz offset rising Gulf exports.
- **Weak breadth.** Decliners outnumbered advancers about 3.7 to 1 on the NYSE and 2.5 to 1 on the Nasdaq. South Korea's Kospi fell 2.6% and Taiwan's Taiex 2.3%.

## Notable company news

- **Chips:** [[AVGO]] rose 2.5% to $341.60, the S&P 500's top gainer, as investors rotated toward Nvidia alternatives; [[MRVL]] bounced 2.3% after Monday's slide. [[MU]] (-0.9%), [[INTC]] (-0.5%; Nvidia's 13F showed it trimmed its Intel stake by a quarter) and [[AMD]] (about flat) recovered from deeper intraday losses. [[TSM]] fell 2.0% in Taipei and [[ASML]] 5.3% in Amsterdam on a new US rule tightening checks on chip foundries. [[KLAC]] and [[ADI]] gained after hours on Nvidia's beat.
- **Megacaps:** [[MSFT]] fell 2.3% to $506.24 with software stocks despite a slate of Copilot launches at Ignite; [[GOOGL]] -1.2% to $280.20, near its 52-week low; [[META]] -1.2% after a $15B bond sale; [[AAPL]] -0.3% amid an iCloud sign-in outage; [[AMZN]] and [[TSLA]] about flat (UBS started Amazon at Buy). [[PLTR]] fell about 3.2%, its fourth straight drop.
- **Earnings:** [[HD]] beat on sales ($43.4B, comps +2.2%) with EPS roughly in line, narrowed its full-year outlook and named CFO Richard McPhail CEO effective Nov 23; shares fell about 2.7%.
- **Health care:** [[UNH]] dropped 5.4% to $389.68, among the Dow's worst, on the CMS proposal. [[LLY]] signed a cancer pact with Stipple Bio worth up to $5.9B; [[MRK]] raised its quarterly dividend to $0.88 and, after the close, ended development of nemtabrutinib; [[ABT]]'s Exact Sciences unit settled its ColoSense patent fight with Geneoscopy; Barclays raised its [[AMGN]] target to $430.
- **Other:** [[CSCO]] rose 0.6%, the Dow's best, after HSBC upgraded it to Buy ($133 target). [[JPM]] fell about 2.7% and named Bregje de Best as its next CFO. TD Cowen raised its [[KO]] target to $112 and Raymond James its [[PG]] target to $185. [[PANW]] slid ahead of Wednesday's report.
- **Not in the vault:** Salesforce -9.5%, Nike -6.3%, Enphase -7.6% (a five-year low), United Airlines -3.7% after a system outage.
