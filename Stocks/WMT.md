## Bull case / bear case

- **Bull:** Commentators cite a strong Q2 beat, growth in ads (+38%), membership (fee revenue up about 17%) and marketplace income, AI shopping tools such as Sparky and a roughly $6B Symbotic robotics expansion, a record of beating estimates in each of the last four quarters (TradingKey), rotation from tech into retailers lifting the stock toward its highs (Stocks Today), peers Target, Home Depot, Lowe's and TJX mostly beating estimates this week (AOL), and pre-earnings target raises from UBS ($149, Buy), JPMorgan ($145, Overweight), BofA ($139, Buy), BMO ($136, Outperform), Mizuho ($135, Outperform) and Wells Fargo ($135, Overweight), plus Bernstein (Buy, $142) and BTIG (Buy, $140); per Koyfin data cited by Stocktwits, 38 of 43 analysts rate it Buy or Strong Buy.
- **Bear:** Commentators flag a valuation of roughly 38-46x earnings against single-digit revenue growth (about 40x forward vs. Target's ~17x, per a Nov 18 Yahoo Finance column), lukewarm pre-report sentiment with options pricing a ~6% swing (AOL), overbought technical readings into the report (Investing.com) and Hightower's view that the stock is too expensive (Talk Business), a sub-1% dividend yield, an average analyst target (about $129, per Koyfin) roughly at the share price, Trefis's warning that about $2.9B of one-time tariff refunds flattered recent profit growth with Q3 operating income guided up only 2-4%, expected slowing in U.S. comps (consensus 2.9% vs. 4.5% a year ago) and BMO's cut to its fiscal 2028 comp forecast on drug-pricing headwinds, FTC formal orders on personalized pricing, pressure on lower-income shoppers, more than $2B of added fuel-related costs (24/7 Wall St., which notes one Sell rating among 43 analysts), and insider share sales.
